# Training Videos

Three sets of short screen-recorded tutorials teach non-developers to use the
tools the department has handed them: the content dashboard, and the two
Aladawy WordPress sites. **Every video is captioned in Arabic, burned into the
picture**, because the people who watch them work in Arabic and a caption
that is part of the frame cannot be switched off, lost, or left behind when the
file is forwarded on WhatsApp.

| Set | For | Videos | Total | Recorded on |
| --- | --- | ------ | ----- | ----------- |
| [Content dashboard](#content-dashboard) | Everyone with a dashboard login; one video for admins | 5 | ~7 min | The online dashboard in practice mode |
| [Aladawy Shop](#aladawy-shop) | The shop's customers (part 1) and its staff (part 2) | 6 | ~14 min | `staging.aladawyshop.com` |
| [Aladawy Pack](#aladawy-pack) | Aladawy Pack's staff who run the website | 6 | ~18 min | A test copy of the WordPress site |

<Callout type="info">
  **The videos are not in any repository**, and this page deliberately does not
  link to them. They are large binary files that change whenever a screen
  changes, which is the wrong shape for git, and some are for a client's staff
  rather than for us. The web department holds them. To get a copy, or to send
  one to a client, [ask](/help/who-to-ask).
</Callout>

Five Arabic videos for the [content dashboard](/repositories/adawy-dashboard)
at `admin.adawygroup.com`. Each is silent, 720p, and captioned step by step.
The first four are for **everyone**, including content creators; the fifth is
for **admins**.

| # | Video | Length | Covers |
| - | ----- | ------ | ------ |
| 1 | Signing in and passwords | 1:11 | The sign-in page, switching the interface between Arabic and English, showing and hiding the password, "forgot password", the Sites page, the practice-mode banner, signing out |
| 2 | Editing text and publishing | 1:52 | Opening a site, its sections, editing a field in Arabic and English, the "edited" marker, the placeholders and tags that must stay intact (and how the dashboard blocks a publish when one is deleted), undoing one field, searching all text, Review, then Publish |
| 3 | Replacing images | 1:12 | The Images tab, Replace, cropping the new photo to the old one's shape, the "new" marker, undoing before publishing, reviewing old and new side by side, publishing |
| 4 | History and undo | 0:52 | The History tab — who published what and when — and undoing a whole publish, which reverts only what that publish changed |
| 5 | Users and roles *(admins)* | 1:53 | The Users page, Admin vs Content creator, inviting someone, the set-password link, the password rules and strength meter, changing a role, re-sending a link, removing a user — and the invitee's side of it. Also the two WordPress cards that open each Aladawy site's `wp-admin` |

**Recorded in practice mode** (27 September 2026). Every publish in the videos
went to the practice repository, not to a website — which is why the amber
practice banner is on screen. When the dashboard goes live the screens stay the
same and a publish reaches the real site within a minute or two, so going live
does **not** by itself make these videos stale. The banner will be missing, and
nothing else changes.

## When a video goes stale

A tutorial is wrong the day the screen it shows changes, and nothing warns
anyone — the same failure that keeps screenshots out of this handbook, except a
video cannot be fixed with a one-line edit. So **when a pull request changes
something a video shows, note "re-record video N" on its card** — the person
changing the screen is the only one who knows, at that moment, that a video
just went wrong.

What makes each set stale:

| Set | Stale when… | Not stale when… |
| --- | ----------- | --------------- |
| Content dashboard | A label, button, tab or step changes on sign-in, Sites, the editor, Images, History or Users; the password rules change; a site card is added or removed in a way a video shows | The dashboard goes live (only the practice banner disappears); a new site is added to the registry |
| Aladawy Shop | The theme's catalogue, product, quote or checkout flow changes; delivery-fee or cash-on-delivery rules change; the order statuses or the Settings → Aladawy screen change; a major WordPress or WooCommerce update reshapes `wp-admin` | Prices, products or delivery fees change — the videos teach the screens, not the numbers |
| Aladawy Pack | The Site content screen, the product fields or the Enquiries screen change; the public site's menu or quote flow changes; a major WordPress update reshapes `wp-admin` | Wording or products change |

## How they were made, and how to re-record one

All three sets were recorded by **a scripted browser recorder built on
Playwright**: a script drives a real browser through the steps, the screen is
captured to video, and the Arabic captions are burned into the frame
afterwards. Scripting it is the point — a re-record is a re-run, the pacing and
pointer are the same every time, and nothing personal (a real inbox, a saved
password) ends up on camera.

<Callout type="warning">
  **The recorder scripts are not in any repository yet.** Today, re-recording a
  video means rebuilding the script for it. Until the scripts are committed —
  most naturally beside the code each set teaches, in
  [`adawy-dashboard`](/repositories/adawy-dashboard),
  [`aladawy-shop-wp`](/repositories/aladawy-shop-wp) and
  [`aladawy-pack-wp`](/repositories/aladawy-pack-wp) — treat every UI change
  as expensive to document. Closing this gap is worth a card of its own.
</Callout>

When the scripts exist, a re-record follows the same rules the originals did:

1. **Record against practice or staging, never production.** A tutorial clicks
   Publish, places orders and sends emails; on the live system each of those is
   real. The dashboard's practice mode and the shop's staging site exist so
   that nothing a video does reaches a customer.
2. **Use a test account, never a person's.** Accounts, orders and customers on
   camera must be test data, because the video will be forwarded outside the
   department.
3. **Keep the caption language and the style** — Arabic, one short sentence
   per step, burned in — so a re-recorded video sits beside the rest without
   looking like a different product.
4. **Replace the file in the set, keep its number and name**, and tell whoever
   distributed the old one. A stale copy in a client's WhatsApp is still being
   watched.
