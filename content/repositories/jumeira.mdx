# `jumeira`

**Client project.** The pre-launch site for **Jumeira Catering** — a contract
catering and premium buffet company in **Benghazi, Libya**, feeding corporates,
hospitals, oil fields, aviation, conferences and weddings.

Private · standalone Next.js 16 app · not part of the monorepo

It is the second-largest codebase in the org after
[`adawy-platform`](/repositories/adawy-platform), and by some distance the most
demanding one per page: a single route whose entire content is a scroll-driven
film. If you are looking for the department's hardest client engineering, it is
here.

## What it is for

`PRODUCT.md` states the job precisely: *"a pre-launch holding page while the
full site is built… this page is not competing for search traffic, it is
answering the question 'are these people serious?' for someone who already has
the link."* Success is defined as **a contract buyer forwarding the link instead
of asking for a company profile PDF**.

Two audiences with almost nothing in common: a procurement lead deciding whether
Jumeira can feed several hundred people a day on schedule, and a family planning
a wedding, arriving from Instagram on an Arabic phone. That is why the argument
is a film rather than a page of claims.

## Stack

| i18n | next-intl — `ar` (default) + `en`, `localePrefix: 'always'`, `hreflang` `ar-LY` |

No forms, no server actions, no API routes, no state library. The only stateful
things on the page are a scrub position, a theme and an audio element.

## The one idea to understand first

**The whole page is a single GSAP ScrollTrigger pin — 1140% of the viewport —
carrying two films and an ending in one continuous timeline.**

It was built as two pinned sections once, and *"it read as two: the first pin
released, the scene scrolled away and the next scrolled in, so a cut appeared
where the footage had none."* The services film opens on the exact frame the
opening film closes on, which is the one place a pin boundary would show. So
the second scene takes the parent timeline as an argument rather than building
its own.

The scene is split across eleven files **by what the code is, not by how big it
got**: markup / choreography / film-as-media / arithmetic, per half. The three
arithmetic modules import nothing at all, which is exactly what lets them be
tested in Node with no DOM — the same rule the platform applies to its
`lib/` layer ([testing](/standards/testing)).

<Callout type="info">
  Markup and timeline share elements through a `MARKER` constant map, never
  through refs or raw selector strings: *"a GSAP target is a selector and a
  React prop is an attribute, so an animated element is otherwise spelled twice
  with nothing to catch a rename."* A test holds every `data-*` selector in the
  stylesheet to a `MARKER` value. This is the same idea as the platform's typed
  anchors ([platform architecture](/architecture/platform-architecture)).
</Callout>

## Scrubbing video, and what it costs

Nothing on this page ever *plays*. Both films are scrubbed against the
scrollbar, and almost every unusual decision in the repo follows from that.

- **Frame rate is matched to the pin, not to playback.** The opening film is
  10 fps, the services film 24 — *"10 fps is not a playback rate; it is how
  finely the footage is cut against the scrollbar."* Dropping frames was
  measured as a better lever than raising CRF: 10 fps at CRF 26 came out both
  smaller **and** higher quality than 12 fps at CRF 27.
- **Every file is all-intra.** A seek is only as fast as the nearest keyframe
  behind it, and reverse scrubbing is the first thing that falls apart without
  it.
- **The scrub is clamped to what has actually downloaded.** A `<video>` asked
  for a frame it does not hold *neither stalls nor reports anything* — it keeps
  showing its last decoded frame while `currentTime` runs away from it.
  Measured on a throttled phone, unclamped showed **1 frame across 3150 px of
  scroll**; clamped showed **19**.
- **Two cuts × two tiers.** Cuts follow the screen's shape and can change on
  rotation; tiers follow what the connection turns out to carry, are decided
  once, and only ever go down. Every cut of a film holds the same frames at the
  same timestamps, so a rotation or a downshift can restore `currentTime`.
- **The second film is queued behind the first, not merely time-gated.** At low
  bandwidth both films split the line and *"neither film could move and both
  were being paid for."* The rule, stated in the repo: **one film that scrubs
  beats two that do not.**
- **It stays a `<video>`, never a canvas frame sequence.** Measured twice: file
  size is a wash, but `currentTime` seek runs ~2.4 ms on the phone's hardware
  decoder against ~20 ms for a WebP bitmap draw — past the frame budget.

**Nothing downloads until the reader asks for it.** The `<video>` carries no
`src` in the markup and `preload="none"`, because Chrome pulled 6.8 MB just
deciding what the film was under `preload="metadata"` — on a page that is
otherwise about half a megabyte.

<Callout type="warning">
  **Do not measure media with Resource Timing.**
  `performance.getEntriesByType` does not surface media range requests, so it
  reports 0 bytes for a film and looks fine while CI fails. That mistake is how
  the 6.8 MB shipped in the first place.
</Callout>

## Why there is no Lenis here

Every other Next.js project in the org pairs GSAP with Lenis. This one removed
it, and `scroll-behavior: smooth` is banned outright.

The page is one pinned scene, so **nothing visibly scrolls during the pin**
except the scrubbed timeline, and ScrollTrigger's own `scrub: 0.5` catch-up
already produces the glide. Lenis added a second scroll authority whose lerp
stacked on top of the scrub and read as seek lag.

*Why this matters beyond one repo:* smooth scroll is a house default, not a
house law. When a page's scroll *is* the animation, the second authority is the
bug ([UI standards](/standards/ui)).

## Arabic is the default, and the design language

`i18n/routing.ts` says it plainly: *"Arabic is the default, because the
business, its clients and its staff are in Benghazi."* `PRODUCT.md` goes
further — *"every type decision is judged in Arabic first. A treatment that is
beautiful in Latin caps and merely acceptable in Arabic has failed the majority
of the audience."*

Two consequences worth carrying to your own work:

- **Physical direction utilities are lint errors here even inside
  `components/ui/`** — the vendored primitives are held to the same rule as
  everything else, because a shadcn default that ships `ml-2` mirrors wrongly
  in Arabic exactly like your own code would ([i18n & RTL](/standards/i18n-rtl)).
- **Locale facts live in one module.** `HREFLANG` is a `Record<Locale, string>`
  rather than a `locale === 'ar' ? 'ar-LY' : locale` written out in three files.
  Adding a third locale is now a **type error until it is mapped**, instead of
  three silent `else` branches.

<Callout type="warning">
  **A number inside Arabic prose needs its own bidi isolate — isolating a
  parent is not enough.** Space-separated digit groups reorder, so a phone
  number renders back-to-front. Nothing on the holding page prints a number
  today, which means this bites the moment the contact block lands.
</Callout>

<details>
<summary>Traps that have already cost someone a day</summary>

- **The intro curtain overlay must never be removed from the DOM.** The
  controller called `overlay.remove()` once; React still held the fiber, and
  the next client-side navigation died in the unmount commit with no error
  boundary above the layout. The symptom was *switching locale showed an error
  page, every time, in both directions.* Retire it by dropping its attribute
  and letting CSS hide it.
- **The curtain's release chain must never be able to reject.**
  `Animation.finished` rejects with `AbortError` when its element leaves the
  DOM, and `Promise.race` settles on the first settlement — including a
  rejection — so the timeout ceiling cannot rescue it and the reader gets a
  page that will not scroll.
- **Anything the curtain hides needs its own CSS failsafe keyframe.** The
  header had none, and a fixed transparent strip went on swallowing clicks
  whenever a release never came.
- **Check curtain timing on a throttled network, not a local load.** The
  truncation only appears once first paint passes 300 ms, which is every real
  connection and no local one. CPU throttling barely moves it.
- **`requestVideoFrameCallback` is not trustworthy under CPU throttling** — it
  under-reports badly enough to look like a catastrophic frame drop that is not
  happening. Track `currentTime` against scroll instead.
- **Re-encode from the masters, never from the files in `public/`.** Deriving
  the light cuts from the full cuts cost measurable quality across all four.
  After any re-encode, check that keyframe count still equals frame count in
  all eight files, or all-intra has been lost and reverse scrubbing with it.

</details>

## Going live

Same publishing switch as the other client projects: `PRODUCTION_HOST` set in
**both** `lib/site.ts` and `next.config.ts`, hostname only. Setting one and not
the other ships a half-indexed site — the meta tags and the `X-Robots-Tag`
header disagree — and a colocated test is the only thing that catches it. Full
procedure: [Client Projects](/how-we-work/client-projects#the-go-live-switch).

Two things this repo does that the generic checklist does not cover:

- **The site URL falls back to a hard-coded pre-launch host, not an env var.**
  CI runs `vercel build` on a GitHub runner where no `VERCEL_*` host variables
  exist, so the env-var version read back `undefined`, every page prerendered
  with `metadataBase` on `localhost`, and link cards rendered with no image.
- **A redirect map from the client's legacy domain is a launch blocker.** Only
  a handful of entry points are mapped today; the rest has to come off the old
  sitemap before the domain moves ([why](/how-we-work/client-projects#launch-checklist)).
