# Architecture Decision Records

Significant technical decisions, recorded so future team members understand
not just *what* we decided but *why* — and what we knew at the time. Format:
**Context → Decision → Consequences.**

## How to add one

**The trigger:** a decision that closes off a plausible alternative, and that
someone will question again in a year. Anything smaller is a
[standards](/standards) page or a code comment — an ADR set that records
routine choices stops being read, which costs more than the missing record
would have.

Write it in the same three parts, add it to the index above, and open a PR.
State the trade-off you accepted in **Consequences**; a record with no cost in
it is a record nobody believes. If a later decision changes one, do not delete
it — mark it superseded and say what replaced it, because the reasoning that
was wrong is the most useful thing in the file.

## ADR-001 — One monorepo: a thin app per brand on shared packages

- **Context:** 38 domains today, 110 planned. Fully separate sites mean every
  change, patch, and feature ×38 — roughly 30 engineers at maturity. A single
  multi-tenant app with domain routing was considered, but it couples every
  brand to one deployment and makes flagship-level customization awkward.
- **Decision:** One Turborepo monorepo for **Adawy Group's own brands**
  ([ADR-010](#adr-010--group-brands-share-a-monorepo-client-projects-get-their-own-repo)
  scopes this — client projects do not live here). Each brand is a **thin
  Next.js app** (`apps/group`, `apps/studio`, `apps/pack`, …) deployed to its
  own domain as its own Vercel project. All real engineering lives in shared
  packages: `@adawy/ui` (shadcn primitives), `@adawy/design` (design tokens),
  `@adawy/motion` (GSAP + Lenis), `@adawy/i18n` (locales and direction),
  `@adawy/site` (identity, routes, metadata) and `@adawy/holding`. Own apps are
  for flagship brands; the long tail is served by one multi-tenant app
  ([ADR-008](#adr-008--flagships-get-their-own-app-the-long-tail-shares-one-multi-tenant-app)).
- **Consequences:** A fix in a shared package ships to every brand from one
  PR; Turborepo rebuilds only affected apps. Each brand deploys independently —
  a bad deploy on one site cannot take down the others, and flagships keep
  full creative freedom in their own app. Trade-offs: a shared-package change
  re-deploys every affected app, and discipline is required to keep apps thin —
  reusable code must flow upstream into `packages/`, never get forked into an
  app ([project structure](/standards/project-structure)).

## ADR-002 — Rebuild on a code-first stack instead of patching WordPress

- **Context:** The 2026 technical audit of adawygroup.com documented 20
  findings (Lighthouse mobile ~32, template footer credits, broken sub-brand
  links, missing SEO/meta/structured data, stock photography, English only).
  Root cause: page-builder platform fit, not execution.
- **Decision:** Rebuild from scratch on Next.js, accepted by
  leadership. WordPress is right for a small business with simple needs and
  wrong for a 38-company group operating one coordinated digital experience.
- **Consequences:** Full code/content ownership, real version control and
  review, bilingual/RTL by design, cinematic motion possible. Cost: we build
  and own everything — hence the design system investment in Phase 1.

## ADR-003 — Keep Odoo as the system of record

- **Context:** The group runs finance, inventory, payroll, HR, and
  manufacturing on Odoo. Custom ERPs almost always end in regret.
- **Decision:** Keep Odoo permanently. Build differentiating layers (portal,
  dashboards, AI) on top via its APIs, behind an abstraction layer; cache in
  our PostgreSQL.
- **Consequences:** Years of engineering saved. Odoo API deprecation (Odoo 22,
  late 2028) costs one or two sprints thanks to the abstraction. SAP is a
  Phase-4 evaluation, not a today problem — and the platform doesn't depend on
  Odoo specifically.

## ADR-004 — Defer the CMS; content is managed in the repo for now

- **Context:** A unified admin dashboard (originally planned on Payload CMS)
  was considered for Phase 1 so non-developers could edit every brand's
  content. With a two-person team and the website launch as the priority,
  building and operating a CMS now adds scope without near-term benefit.
- **Decision:** Defer the CMS. Site content lives in the repository (MDX and
  configuration), edited by the team and reviewed via PR like any other
  change. Revisit when content volume or non-developer editing demands it —
  and if adopted, it will be self-hosted, never a per-seat hosted service.
- **Consequences:** Zero CMS infrastructure to run today; every content change
  is versioned and reviewed. Trade-off: content edits require a developer in
  the loop until a CMS decision is made.

## ADR-005 — Aladawy Shop ships as a quote request, not a checkout

- **Context:** Aladawy Shop is a **client** of the department
  ([ADR-010](#adr-010--group-brands-share-a-monorepo-client-projects-get-their-own-repo)),
  not a Group brand. It ran on WooCommerce. The obvious brief was "rebuild the
  store", but the business does not actually sell at a list price: packaging is
  quoted by grade, and the final figure is settled by a human against live
  stock. A cart and a checkout would have modelled a transaction that does not
  exist, and a hosted platform (Shopify) would have charged a subscription plus
  transaction fees for a checkout nobody uses.
- **Decision:** Build a custom storefront whose checkout is a **quote
  request** — no account, no payment, no database. Every product carries a
  price band per grade (economy / standard / premium); the running figure is
  always a range, never a single number. The catalogue is committed TypeScript;
  submissions leave through one delivery seam.
- **Consequences:** The site matches how the business actually sells, and there
  is no payment surface to secure or subscription to pay. The code is shaped so
  commerce can arrive without a rewrite: the quote slice already carries
  everything an order needs, and the API route already validates the full
  payload server-side. Trade-off: this is deliberately *not* the reusable
  commerce engine the group might one day want for its own brands — that
  remains an open decision, to be made when a Group brand actually needs to
  take payment.

<Callout type="info">
  **Superseded scope.** An earlier version of this record committed the group
  to a shared custom-commerce platform (catalog synced from Odoo, Paymob +
  Fawry) with Aladawy Shop as its first tenant. That framing was wrong on the
  premise: Aladawy Shop is a client project, so it cannot be the group's
  commerce platform. Group commerce is unscheduled and will get its own ADR
  when a Group brand needs it.
</Callout>

## ADR-006 — Every brand keeps its own domain, cross-linked to the group

- **Context:** Three options: subdomains of adawygroup.com, fully separate
  domains, or own domains with cross-linking.
- **Decision:** Own domains with "part of Adawy Group" cross-links both ways
  (the LVMH model).
- **Consequences:** Each company reads as a real company, not a corporate
  department; the ecosystem stays discoverable. Each domain points at its
  brand app's own Vercel project (Cloudflare in front).

## ADR-007 — Documentation lives in this repo, public, docs-as-code

- **Context:** Department knowledge was scattered across chats and PDFs; new
  members had no single source of truth.
- **Decision:** This Nextra site, public, on the same Vercel/GitHub workflow
  as everything else. Changes via PR.
- **Consequences:** Onboarding is self-serve; docs rot is visible in git; the
  handbook itself demonstrates the platform's standards. The handbook is
  **English-only by decision, not omission**: its audience is engineers, whose
  working language is English, and a parallel Arabic tree would rot with a
  small team — a stale translation is worse than none. Revisit if the
  audience expands beyond engineers (company-wide operations handbook, or a
  hire for whom English documentation is a genuine barrier) — and then
  translate the process pages (How We Work, onboarding), not the ADRs.
  Arabic stays first-class where it serves the market: the brand sites.

## ADR-008 — Flagships get their own app; the long tail shares one multi-tenant app

- **Context:** ADR-001 gives each brand its own Next.js app. That is right for
  flagships, but extrapolated to 110 brands it breaks down: a change to
  `@adawy/ui` would trigger ~110 Vercel builds (turbo-ignore cannot help — the
  change genuinely affects all of them), and the team would manage ~110 Vercel
  projects, domain configs, and env setups. Realistically, brands #10–#110 are
  templated marketing sites — same sections, different tokens and content.
- **Decision:** Two tiers inside the same monorepo. A Group brand gets **its
  own app** (`apps/group`, `apps/studio`, `apps/pack`, …) when it
  needs unique routes, custom sections, or bespoke motion. Everything else
  lives in **one multi-tenant app** (`apps/sites`) that maps hostname → brand
  config (tokens, content, section composition). Both tiers consume the same
  `@adawy/*` packages, so a brand can graduate from `apps/sites` to its own
  app without a rewrite — it is a promotion path, not a migration.
- **Consequences:** The long tail is one deployment and one Vercel project
  (Vercel serves many domains per project natively); design-system PRs stay
  reviewable. The future CMS (ADR-004) has one obvious consumer instead of a
  hundred. Trade-off: `apps/sites` couples its brands to one deployment — the
  flagship isolation argument from ADR-001 deliberately does not apply to
  templated sites. The decision rule per new brand lives in the
  [launch guide](/guides/launch-new-brand).

## ADR-009 — The Phase-2 backend joins the same monorepo

- **Context:** The NestJS backend (auth, Odoo integration, jobs) could live in
  its own repository or in the platform monorepo. The team is small and the
  frontend and backend share TypeScript contracts (API types, Zod schemas).
- **Decision:** The backend lives in the monorepo as `apps/api`, deployed to
  its own host (Railway → DigitalOcean) rather than Vercel.
- **Consequences:** Shared types between portal frontends and the API are
  imported, not duplicated; one CI setup; one place to look. If a dedicated
  backend team ever needs its own repo, extracting one later is far easier
  than merging repos later.

## ADR-010 — Group brands share a monorepo; client projects get their own repo

- **Context:** The department builds two different kinds of software. **Group
  work** is Adawy Group's own brands, where a shared design system pays for
  itself across dozens of sites ([ADR-001](#adr-001--one-monorepo-a-thin-app-per-brand-on-shared-packages)).
  **Client work** is software built for other businesses — Aladawy Pack,
  Aladawy Shop, Jumeira Catering, Zad Al-Hkoul and Baron. Early on these were
  treated as the same thing, which put client sites on the Group roadmap and
  made the monorepo rules look like they applied to everything.
- **Decision:** Two patterns, chosen by *who owns the outcome*.
  - **Group brands** live in `adawy-platform` as apps on shared `@adawy/*`
    packages. ADR-001 and [ADR-008](#adr-008--flagships-get-their-own-app-the-long-tail-shares-one-multi-tenant-app)
    apply here and nowhere else.
  - **Client projects** get a **standalone repository each**, with no
    dependency on `@adawy/*`. They share the department's *standards* — strict
    TypeScript, bilingual with real RTL, WCAG 2.1 AA, the same review and
    deploy discipline — but no code.
- **Consequences:** A client's site cannot be broken by a Group refactor, and
  a Group refactor is not held hostage by a client's release schedule. The
  client owns a repository that is complete on its own, which is what makes
  handover possible at all. The Group roadmap describes Group products only,
  so a phase plan stops competing with client delivery dates.

  Trade-off: patterns get re-implemented per client rather than imported —
  `lib/enquiry.ts`, the content/messages split, and the `PRODUCTION_HOST`
  switch all exist twice already. That is accepted. Coupling a client's
  deliverable to an internal package the client does not own would trade a
  little duplication for a dependency nobody can maintain after handover; the
  shared knowledge is documented in [Client Projects](/how-we-work/client-projects)
  instead of shared in code.

  **Naming hazard:** *Adawy* Pack (a Group brand, `apps/pack`) and *Aladawy*
  Pack (a client, `aladawy-pack`) are different companies one letter apart.
  See [the repository map](/repositories).

  **Open case:** [`adawy-group-links`](/repositories/adawy-group-links) is
  Group-owned and standalone, which this record does not cover. It is a
  microsite rather than a brand site, and nothing in it records why it sits
  outside the platform. Until that is decided the rule reads *Group brand
  **sites** live in the monorepo*, and the microsite is one open case rather
  than a precedent.

## ADR-011 — Feature-first app layout: `features/` + `platform/`

- **Context:** `apps/group` grew to ~60k lines organised as four parallel
  layer trees (`components/`, `lib/`, `scene/`, `hooks/`). Every feature was
  sliced across three or four places under drifting names (contact lived in
  four spots under three names; the brand pages nested differently in every
  tree), `lib/` — 280 files — mixed feature logic, app machinery and generic
  utilities in one namespace, and the load-bearing import rules ("lib is
  pure", "scene has no React") were prose, not lint. The public
  site's language and the code's names had diverged: the `/ecosystem` page
  was built from folders named `ecosystem-hero` and `relay`, while
  `ecosystem` named a *home* section.
- **Decision:** One sentence, meant to still be obvious in five years:
  **routes in `app/`, things you can see in `features/`, machinery in
  `platform/`.** A feature is one thing a visitor can point at, named after
  the public site (`features/ecosystem` is the `/ecosystem` page,
  `features/ecosystem-belt` the home card-belt, `features/brands` the
  `/ecosystem/<brand>` pages, `features/stage` the persistent home 3D world —
  deliberately one feature, because its crane/coins/mark travel across
  sections). Each feature is `{components, scene, lib, index.ts}`.
  Cross-cutting machinery is a `platform/` module (`chrome`, `scroll`,
  `sound`, `gl`, `scene`, `seo`, `brand`, `theme`, `perf`, `boot`, `math`,
  `tokens`, `ui`, …). A four-line boundary law is machine-enforced by
  generated `no-restricted-imports` blocks: platform never imports features;
  a feature imports another feature only through its barrel; `app/` composes
  UI via barrels (feature `lib/` stays importable); `lib/` has no React and
  no three.js, `scene/` has no React. Domain-driven design is adopted only
  where it pays: the vocabulary is written down ([glossary](/architecture/glossary)),
  IO keeps its ports (the Mailer), and tactical DDD waits for the Phase-2
  backend where real bounded contexts live.
- **Consequences:** A file's place is guessable from what it is — "the thing
  on the ecosystem page" has one folder — and renaming or deleting a feature
  touches that folder alone. The layer rules now fail CI instead of relying
  on review memory. Forcing the law to hold with zero exceptions surfaced
  every mislabeled shared piece: the scrub/take driver kit and the journey's
  per-section scroll profiles moved to `platform/scroll`, the sound engine to
  `platform/sound`, group data (members, marks, industries) to
  `platform/brand`, five private `lerp`s and three incompatible `clamp`s
  collapsed into `platform/math`, and eight hand-rolled renderer boots into
  one `bootRenderer`. Trade-offs: a cross-feature convenience import is now
  an error whose fix is a deliberate move to platform, and `git blame`
  crosses one large rename boundary (use `--follow`).

## ADR-012 — Machine-enforce a rule wherever the mistake would not look like one

- **Context:** [ADR-011](#adr-011--feature-first-app-layout-features--platform)
  made one app's boundary law machine-enforced, and it worked — the rules now
  fail CI instead of relying on review memory. The same question is open across
  the other seven repositories, where rules like *never use a physical
  direction class*, *never import GSAP directly* and *keep locale files in
  step* were prose. The 2026 audit of the old site is the argument: its
  accessibility failures were, without exception, things a checklist had been
  supposed to catch. With one reviewer and eight repositories, a rule that
  lives only in a document is a rule that gets skipped on the busy week.
- **Decision:** Generalise ADR-011's enforcement beyond `apps/group`, with an
  explicit test for *what* to enforce: **would this mistake look like a
  mistake to a reviewer scanning the diff?** If yes, prose is enough. If it
  renders identically whether it is right or wrong, it becomes a lint rule or a
  test. Already in force on that basis: physical Tailwind classes, direct
  `gsap` imports and locale-unaware `next/link` imports are lint **errors**;
  message parity, a `tel:` matching its displayed number, the Arabic spelling
  of the brand, and a publishing switch set in both of the two files that need
  it are **tests**; accessibility is an axe-core gate over every route. The
  lint philosophy that follows: **a rule is an error or it is not enabled** —
  no warnings, because a warning-only lint is no lint at all.
- **Consequences:** The rules in this handbook are, for the most part, not
  advice — the build tells you, and review time goes to what no rule can catch:
  naming, structure, whether a section earns its place. Three costs are
  accepted. Adding a rule is slower than writing a sentence, so only rules
  worth that cost get written. A badly scoped rule blocks legitimate work,
  which is why every restriction carries its replacement in its own error
  message. And the coverage is currently uneven — the axe gate runs on
  `apps/group` alone, and one client repo has no CI to hang any of this on
  ([the matrix](/repositories#what-the-repos-actually-share)). Closing those is
  the work this record commits to, not something it claims is done.
